<p>I have decided to start a blog. </p>
<p>It seemed appropriate to cite Genesis for the first post, although I am not religious, whatever that means, given that I do have values. The reasons for doing this are; in their order of relative importance.</p>
<p><strong>Writing</strong><strong><br></strong>As I, and many others have become proficient with LLM tools, pure writing has, for me, become a luxury product. Currently the value of my text output has become detached from its clarity and elegance, as of right now the most effective technique of working with the LLMs is &quot;word salad-ing&quot; and in return we get back &quot;Claude-speak&quot; as the tech people are calling it. Hopefully it doesn&#39;t deteriorate into newspeak, and then leading to doublethink, which I would rather avoid.<br>So, the blog will be a knife sharpener for a skill that is of utmost importance.</p>
<p><strong>Recording</strong><br>If I&#39;m reading this in the future then, this post was clearly a child of her time. Hopefully without even trying, these posts will provide a snapshot of me and my world at that time.</p>
<p>Here is a fun NYT connections<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="eec041dd-d364-44f5-891e-0dae03f356df" data-locator=""><a href="#ref-43e13b07-d58c-433a-8f7a-22dd28327898-2" aria-label="Reference 2" title="">[2]</a></sup> ; Terence Tao, Paul Graham and Patrick Collison what is the connection between them? Aside from being people I think are worth learning from, is that they share a passion for writing blogs<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="8cff3730-315f-4121-aecc-e5aac2a57165" data-locator=""><a href="#ref-43e13b07-d58c-433a-8f7a-22dd28327898-3" aria-label="Reference 3" title="">[3]</a></sup> . Why? Well, who knows, a guess is that intellectual credibility can be earned by sharing good ideas and making good predictions. A prescient text is a way of passively earning these intellectual points, but the inverse is also very much also true. My posts will be timestamped for this reason, so that when I look back, I cannot deceive myself of being prescient on something I was not.<br>Recently I read on a solar panel connoisseur&#39;s and potentially a great entrepreneur&#39;s blog about hiring employees saying: &quot;But I’ve never heard of you and nor have the LLMs. This is a dangerous position to be in.&quot;<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="86dda42f-846c-4ed3-a27f-73e0c6960cdd" data-locator="Section: Create a paper trail of deep expertise and thoughtfulness"><a href="#ref-43e13b07-d58c-433a-8f7a-22dd28327898-4" aria-label="Reference 4, Section: Create a paper trail of deep expertise and thoughtfulness" title="Section: Create a paper trail of deep expertise and thoughtfulness">[4]</a></sup> This is true, so I better get started.</p>
<p><strong>Showing things</strong></p>
<p>Generally, I just get so obsessed about things but then end up forgetting. For example, today STEM kids including me, all are in awe by the Tesla compressed gas car <span class="text-citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="4263b633-6265-4f75-8e2c-dfb04ddd6bc3" data-locator="" data-label="demo" data-label-style="normal"><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7p5LxFdc-Qg" title="The Ramp | Roadster">demo</a></span>.<sup class="citation explanation-marker" contenteditable="false" data-note="505e8f15-3a4a-4f41-93de-4fed8c9d7a2f"><a href="#ref-43e13b07-d58c-433a-8f7a-22dd28327898-note-1" aria-label="Explanation 1">*</a></sup> These cool things or interesting ideas should be remembered, for these are usually the seeds for inspiration.</p>
<p>In future posts I will hopefully include some important graphs, amazing pictures and my commentary on things. Which should be much more niche than today&#39;s shiny object shared with the class :)</p>
