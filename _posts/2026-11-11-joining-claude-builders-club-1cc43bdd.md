<p>Recently I joined the Claude Builders Club at Imperial<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="imperial-claude-builders" data-locator="First general meeting recap"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-1" aria-label="Reference 1, First general meeting recap" title="First general meeting recap">[1]</a></sup> to help organise the hackathon this March, with a budget north of ten thousand pounds, which is exciting. The people who use ai regularly tend to overuse it, and the vast amount of people seem to underuse it.</p>
<p>In 1984, Apple released the Macintosh<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="apple-macintosh-1984" data-locator="24 January 1984 press release"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-2" aria-label="Reference 2, 24 January 1984 press release" title="24 January 1984 press release">[2]</a></sup>, which perfected the graphical user interface (GUI). Instead of typing arcane commands to use a computer, now people could just point and click, and the computer would run the equivalent commands underneath. This made computers actually usable for a wider public. Today as we are slowly transitioning into the language user interface (LUI) era, where we use natural language to command the computer to do stuff, it seems very important to encourage people to use computers in this way. </p>
<p>The organising principle in the GUI era was the window, and now it is the project folder. In the past year, I have been creating a folder and then plugging a USB cable into my computer connected with relevant devices I need. In labs, I just ask the LLM app: &quot;Figure out what is connected via USB and create a dashboard for me to collect and save data, plot in real time.&quot; This has always worked, including with third-party hardware. In this case, it was the little <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://sjaakiechen.github.io/personal-blog/assets/evidence/joining-claude-builders-club/waves-apparatus-annotated.png">detector</a> below the guitar string<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="pasco-sonometer-fig1" data-locator="Introduction and Fig. 1"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-3" aria-label="Reference 3, Introduction and Fig. 1" title="Introduction and Fig. 1">[3]</a></sup>. The AI figured out how to extract its magnetic field, which is proportional to the velocity of the guitar string<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="guadagnin-magnetic-pickup" data-locator="Abstract"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-4" aria-label="Reference 4, Abstract" title="Abstract">[4]</a></sup>, with the signal shown in my ai-created <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://sjaakiechen.github.io/personal-blog/assets/evidence/joining-claude-builders-club/waves-recorder.png">UI</a> for data collection. During any project as it organically gains context, that information gets stored inside files in the folder in notes, code, graphs, etc.<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="anthropic-project-memory" data-locator="CLAUDE.md vs auto memory"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-5" aria-label="Reference 5, CLAUDE.md vs auto memory" title="CLAUDE.md vs auto memory">[5]</a></sup>, and this allows for &quot;continual learning&quot; and &quot;interchangeable LLM models&quot;<sup class="citation" contenteditable="false" data-reference="agents-md-portable-project-context" data-locator="One AGENTS.md works across many agents"><a href="#ref-1cc43bdd-56c6-470e-b6d2-a6c54594c980-6" aria-label="Reference 6, One AGENTS.md works across many agents" title="One AGENTS.md works across many agents">[6]</a></sup>. <br><br>I am inclined to make the hackathon, which will be hosted across universities here in London, to be more of a battle bot–type event, since now hardware is so easily programmable. The condition for winning a robot competition is much more objective and a spectacle to watch compared to hackathon judges.</p>
